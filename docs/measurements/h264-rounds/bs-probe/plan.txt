Deblocking: the boundary-strength derivation as a vector kernel. Probe plan, written before any measurement of this
probe.

Question: what is the most time a kernel that derives a macroblock's boundary strengths (bS, clause 8.7.2.1) in
vector registers could save? The derivation in view is `strengths` in deblock.rs and everything it calls: the
neighbours' records and heads (`Side::new`, `Side::blocks`), the masks of coefficients (`column`, `row`, `spread`),
the motion comparisons (`same_motion`, `split_edge`, `edge_motion`, `split_inside`), the out-of-line
`split_strengths` and `differing_motion_strength`, and `deblock_row`'s test of the result for all zeros. A kernel
would take the macroblock's and its neighbours' records and motion and return the same 32 strengths; it replaces
this set and adds its own instructions, so the bound below, which takes the whole set as removed, is generous on
purpose. Nothing is built for the probe. It bounds only the part such a change removes ("For later probes: what a
doubled call prices"): the slice and neighbour lookups before the call (which slice filter, which neighbours are
usable), the QPs, thresholds and the kernels' calls are kept and not counted.

Build: main at acf3ee2f, `CARGO_PROFILE_DIST_DEBUG=line-tables-only cargo build --locked --profile dist --example
decode -p coreav-h264` (`prof-main`). All 32 training streams at the class frame counts (sd 1800, 720p 900, 1080p 300,
4k 150), `--threads 1 --discard`.

1. Time: perf cpu-clock at 10 kHz, pinned to CPU 2, five runs a stream with `--kernels avx2` (so that the sampled and
   simulated runs execute the same instructions; Valgrind has no AVX-512), and five with the detected kernels
   (AVX-512 on this host; only their total time is used).
2. Instructions and misses: callgrind --dump-instr=yes --cache-sim=yes --D1=49152,12,64, `--kernels avx2`, one run,
   after the perf runs.
3. Per source line of our code (innermost inline frame in a CoreAV crate, the MB layout probe's method): time share,
   instruction share, excess = time share - instruction share, simulated D1 write misses.

The removed set R (deblock.rs at acf3ee2f): every instruction with an inline frame, or in an out-of-line function, at
deblock.rs lines 22 to 361 (from `differing_motion_strength`'s documentation to the end of `split_inside`: the
derivation and all its helpers), and the instructions inlined into `deblock_row` at its lines 626 (the call of
`strengths`) and 633 (the test for all zeros). Its parts, for information:
  R:differing  an instruction with a frame of `differing_motion_strength`;
  R:split      else, one with a frame of `split_strengths` (a macroblock or a neighbour with more than one motion);
  R:strengths  the rest at lines 22 to 361 and line 626 (`strengths`, `edge_strengths`, `Side`, the masks);
  R:zero test  line 633.

Bound per stream, generous on purpose (the MB layout probe's formula, as the setup probe used it):
  I = instruction share of R;
  S = max( sum over R of positive excess,
           W x sum over every line with stores of positive excess ),
      W = R's simulated D1 write misses over all simulated D1 write misses;
  bound = I + S, in % of the stream's sampled AVX2 time;
  bound, detected = bound x (AVX2 time / detected-kernel time). This is the reading the threshold applies to.

Threshold (an earlier change's, as the setup probe applied it): build the kernel round only if, in at least one class, the median
per-stream bound, detected, is 1.5% or more; the round's own bars, committed before its run, decide what is reached.
Otherwise the probe is recorded and nothing is built.

Also reported (information): the bound per part and per class, R's instructions a macroblock, and the bound without
the store-excess term.
