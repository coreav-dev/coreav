CAVLC slice loop in a function of its own (written before the build and before any timing).

Why: the CAVLC slice loop round (docs/design/h264.md, "CAVLC round: the slice loop, runs of skipped macroblocks and one
bit window a slice") met its gain bar on the CAVLC streams (CAVLC 6 0.9802, 6f7799768413 0.9716, the 15 held-out CAVLC
streams 0.9680) and was rejected because the 26 CABAC training streams read 1.0010 (discard) and 1.0024 (write), which
moved the all-32 write, 4k and held-out medians over their bars. Its CABAC functions were identical in machine code;
the per-slice dispatch (decode_slice_data, into which the CAVLC slice loop is inlined) changed, and with it the
placement of the code after it.

Question: with the CAVLC slice loop out of line in a function of its own, so that the CABAC path's functions are
byte-identical to base and the dispatch is a call, does the CAVLC gain hold while the CABAC streams read unchanged?

Base: main at the branch point (82a23b66; its decoder is #507's, dc468a95). k: this branch's code commit.

Code (C): the rejected round's design (runs of skipped macroblocks; one cavlc::Window a slice), rebuilt on main, with
the CAVLC slice loop as an #[inline(never)] function of its own. Requirements checked before any timing, reported in
this directory:
- R1 code: the normalized disassembly of every function of the dist build except the CAVLC slice loop's own and the
  per-slice dispatch is identical to base's; the dispatch differs only in calling the CAVLC slice loop.
- R2 placement: the start addresses of the CABAC path's hot functions (the CABAC slice loop, the residual, the
  macroblock functions, motion compensation and the kernels it calls) modulo 64 in base and in C, reported; where
  they differ, the build is changed to keep them if it can be done without touching CABAC code, and the result is
  reported either way.
- R3 instructions: callgrind of C against base on the 26 CABAC streams at a tenth of the class frame counts: each
  within 0.01% of base; on the six CAVLC streams at the class frame counts: the change reported (the rejected round:
  a median of -4.34%).
- R4 output: identical to base on the 32 streams at 1 and 8 threads (md5 at a tenth of the class frame counts), with
  and without fast on the six CAVLC streams; conformance 97 of 97 at 1, 2 and 8 threads, with scalar kernels, and
  without fast.

Local timing (after R1 to R4): ab3.py, 12 rounds, the 32 training streams at the class frame counts, one thread,
--discard, pinned to one CPU, order rotated each round, CPU time; per stream the median of C over base, bootstrap
95% interval of the median (abq.py). Host: this development VM (directional). A gating run is requested only if all
of these hold:
- CAVLC 6: median at most 0.985, upper end below 1.000;
- CABAC 26 (unchanged code): median between 0.997 and 1.003;
- all 32: median at most 1.002.
Otherwise the code goes to a rejected/ branch and the result is recorded.

Gating bars (fixed now; copied into the design note before the gating run). k over base, median per-stream ratio,
95% bootstrap interval, scripts/h264_parity.py with --null-arm base and the host-health gate, main's harness at the
run; one-thread calls pinned on the 2.90 GHz class first; the eight-thread call only if SF-1 to SF-5 pass, on the
GCP c4-standard-16, one thread a core.
- SF-1 (gain): one thread, discard, the six CAVLC streams: at most 0.990, upper end below 1.000.
- SF-2: one thread, all 32, discard and write: at most 1.003 in each.
- SF-3: one thread, each class (sd, 720p, 1080p, 4k), discard and write: at most 1.005 in each.
- SF-4: one thread, the 26 CABAC streams, discard and write: at most 1.003 in each.
- SF-5: one thread, discard, the 64 held-out streams (docs/measurements/h264-n2-heldout/list.json): at most 1.003.
- SF-6 (guard): eight threads, discard, all 32: at most 1.005; each class at most 1.010.
- SF-7: output identical to base on the 32 training streams at 1 and 8 threads, with and without fast; conformance
  as in R4.
Why 1.003 and not 1.000 for SF-2, SF-4 and SF-5: 26 of the 32 training streams and 49 of the 64 held-out streams run
code identical to base's, so those medians sit at the null. A bar at 1.000 would be decided by the null's noise: the
rejected round's unchanged CABAC streams read 1.0010 and 1.0024. 1.003 still fails a CABAC slowdown of a third of a
per cent. Reported as information: the ratio to FFmpeg 9.0.2, overall, per class, the six CAVLC streams and
6f7799768413, with the share of streams faster; the 15 held-out CAVLC streams' median.
One valid run decides; a re-run of the same arms only for a recorded host fault.

Addendum (after R1 to R4 on 82a23b66, before any timing):
- R2 was not met on 82a23b66 (r2-placement.txt): 10 of 65 CABAC hot functions moved by 48 bytes modulo 64. The
  function order follows symbol names and .text's start moves with the read-only data, so no CAVLC-only change kept
  every one; the closest variant kept 58 of 65 and cost a third of the CAVLC instruction cut.
- In that build, fast::decision::TABLES (8-byte entries, alignment 2) moved from 0 to 4 modulo 8, so one entry in eight
  straddles a cache line in the DecodeDecision site, where base's placement keeps every entry inside a line by
  accident. C therefore also gives Tables #[repr(C, align(64))]: no entry straddles a line whatever the layout. It is
  the one change outside the CAVLC code, a placement attribute on read-only data; the site's code and its results
  do not change, and SF-4 (the CABAC streams) judges it with the rest.
- #498 (the CABAC mvd round) merged after the plan was written. The round is rebased onto main 3f918b38, which is
  base from now on, and R1 to R4 are run again there before any timing. The bars SF-1 to SF-7 and the local timing's
  condition do not change.
